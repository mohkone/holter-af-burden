# Fixed automatic-RR detector

The rhythm detector uses only automatic ventricular peak times and the recording clock. It reads original `.hea` and `.qrs` streams for all 84 LTA and all 128 SHDB recordings. The evaluation joins to available rhythm-reference records separately. The detector neither reads a reference rhythm file nor consults patient metadata. On LTA the QRS series contains output from a beat detector and additional manually inserted AF-termination events. Only the standard N event (code 1) contributes a peak. All other types, including manual T (code 19) and artifact markers (16), are excluded. Timestamp accumulation includes the elapsed time represented by excluded annotations. On SHDB the paper specifies epltd automatic R-peak detection. Both files contain peaks before the manual rhythm adjudication; automatic QRS timing in SHDB is shared with the annotation workflow, so the two are not independent measurements of beat timing. In both datasets sample positions are interpreted at the header sampling rate; explicit annotation clock headers, when present, are checked. The pilot uses the two cohorts' original automated QRS detections, which were generated by different algorithms.

## Fixed implementation

We adapted the short-window coefficient of sample entropy (COSEn) of Lake and Moorman. The underlying entropy formula, 12-interval window, m=1, minimum count of five matched templates and threshold −1.4 are literature based. For each window x, use all unordered pairs from the first eleven possible two-interval starting positions. Let B be the number of pairs within tolerance in their first component, A the number of pairs within tolerance in both components. Choose r as the maximum of 30 ms and the fifth order statistic of the pairwise Chebyshev distances. The implementation counts ties at r. Then COSEn = log(B/A) + log(2r / mean(x)). Distances are formed from integer ticks; the formula is invariant to a common change of units if the tolerance floor is converted correctly. Equal comparison at threshold and choice of radius in tied/quantized windows are specified here for reproducibility. No fitting was performed on study outcomes.

The temporal voting and quality rules are adaptations for this pilot, not a replication of the original study's hourly sampling. Query points fall at 5, 15, 25, 35, 45 and 55 seconds in each complete clock minute. A query uses the six intervals before and six intervals after the nearest automatic beat (earlier wins a tie). A valid vote is 1 if COSEn > −1.4 and 0 otherwise. If the center is more than 2.5 s from any beat, fewer than 12 surrounding intervals exist, or any selected interval is outside 0.25–2.50 s, the interrogation votes 0.5, preserving every complete minute. The score equals the mean of six votes; it estimates a fraction of time and is not a calibrated probability. The `invalid_fraction` field records the proportion of undetermined interrogations. The method is retrospective and uses short windows on both sides of interrogation times. An isolated rapid or slow interval, ectopy, gaps, regular flutter and automated QRS errors can affect the predictions.
