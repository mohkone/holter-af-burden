# Confirmation cohort and data provenance

## IRIDIA-AF, release 1.0.1

The primary independent cohort is a Belgian outpatient cardiology collection. A cardiac nurse screened Holters, a cardiologist confirmed AF, and episode boundaries were reviewed and aligned to ECG samples. Patients with an implanted device, persistent/permanent AF, and poor-quality recordings were excluded by the source study. First-day analyses concern segments of recordings from patients selected for paroxysmal AF, including times with zero AF on that day. [Original description](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-02621-1) · [Zenodo release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8405941) · [Source authors’ repository](https://github.com/cedricgilon/iridia-af)

All 167 recordings from 152 patients are included. Exactly one day-0 file is used per recording. The metadata relate the records to 139 patients with one, twelve patients with two, and one patient with four. The prior development source is Northwestern University in the USA, and the previous test source is Saitama Heart Center in Japan. Provenance is institutionally and geographically distinct. There is no shared patient identifier across databases to verify identity linkage. [LTA](https://physionet.org/content/ltafdb/1.0.0/) · [SHDB-AF](https://physionet.org/content/shdb-af/1.0.1/)

The Microport Syneview machine's automatic R-to-R durations are supplied in day-0 HDF5 files and are exact multiples of 5 ms. Beat positions are accumulated on the 200-Hz ECG grid with origin zero. Only this unlabeled automatic sequence and the clock are used by detectors. Manual episode start/stop indices in the separate ECG-label files define the response as exact occupied duration in half-open 60-second minutes. Episodes extending into day 1 are clipped at the day-0 end; the arbitrary split of an episode at a minute boundary preserves the actual fraction of that minute. In a separate provenance-only check we compared the ECG episode boundary with the two bracketing positions of the matching RR interval in the source's RR-label table. This audit is not used to align, correct, train, or select any predictions or cases. The maximum record-end difference between official first-day duration and automatic RR duration was 1.125 seconds; 563 available paired start/end checks differed by at most 5.07 s from the nearer bracketing point. Absolute timing from cumulative automatic detections remains subject to this source imprecision.

Only complete minutes are retained. Minute zero contains a 30-second device calibration signal and is excluded in every record. Across 237,060 complete minutes, 236,893 remain (range 1,189–1,439 per record). No automatic RR interval exceeded 60 seconds (maximum 46.86 seconds). The publishers inspected and trimmed noisy recording ends before public release. No waveform-quality assessment beyond their quality control is performed. The endpoint is reviewer-marked AF; other rhythms were not exhaustively subtyped in this database.

The 10,532,846,900-byte source ZIP was accessed with HTTP byte ranges. Both the publisher's displayed full-archive MD5 and the public release identifier are recorded. Each of 501 selected members (167 day-0 RR HDF5 files and the ECG/RR label CSVs for each record) was checked against ZIP central-directory CRC-32, uncompressed size and member name, then assigned its own SHA-256 for local replay. The 17,970-byte metadata CSV matches its Zenodo MD5. The entire waveform archive's MD5 was not computed. `iridia_source_receipt.json`, `iridia_clock_audit.csv` and `manifest.csv` record source identities and derived-file checksums.

To reconstruct these derived cohort files with NumPy and h5py from the primary archive, run `python new_data.py` in this folder. For offline rebuilding after full selected-member retrieval, run `python new_data.py --offline`. Python 3.12 and h5py were used for HDF5. HDF5 files and rhythm CSVs are distributed under CC BY 4.0. The derived files contain cumulative beats and minute-level fractions constructed from the data of Cédric Gilon and coauthors; use and redistribution require attribution. [Release and contributors](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8405941) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## MIT-BIH Atrial Fibrillation Database 1.0.0, supplemental short-record data

The ambulatory recordings were acquired at Beth Israel Hospital, Boston. The reference uses original manual .atr rhythm transitions and the detector's automatic .qrs files. Inputs do not include the manually corrected .qrsc files. The corrected alignment of the automatic .qrs of record 07859 is part of the current release. Its 1983 source tapes predate IRIDIA and SHDB. A distinct study site does not prove disjoint subjects across databases without individual linkage.

Restrict to 23 waveform-available record IDs; 00735 and 03665 are excluded to avoid assuming acquisition duration from absent waveforms. Source tables give record IDs without person-level crosswalk. Each record is an independent unit for descriptive supplementary analysis, with the absence of a linkage key disclosed. MIT-BIH AFDB records are approximately ten hours and do not directly validate a full 24-hour interval. In three records the authors note missing 10.24-second signal blocks. Consecutive constant two-lead samples in these records and the early-termination record (06453) are measured directly from the published data, and any overlapping full minute is excluded. Rhythm states continue to the confirmed end of recording; no AF duration is imputed before the first label. A long trailing interval without automatic beats is independently flagged. [Database and annotations](https://physionet.org/content/afdb/1.0.0/) · [Source errata](https://physionet.org/files/afdb/1.0.0/notes.txt) · [Original license, ODC-By 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/by/1-0/)

The AFDB supplementary inventory includes 13,688 supported full minutes across 23 record IDs (552–599 per recording). The independent WFDB 4.3.0 audit compared every rhythm transition and automatic N peak to the binary decoder. Source-file SHA-256 digests, missing-block exclusions and derived-file hashes are provided. IRIDIA methods, data and adapted arrays are attributed to Cédric Gilon, Jean-Marie Grégoire, Marianne Mathieu, Stéphane Carlier and Hugues Bersini. The publisher citation for AFDB is Moody GB, Mark RG, “A new method for detecting atrial fibrillation using R-R intervals”, Computers in Cardiology (1983).
