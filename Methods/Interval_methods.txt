Supplementary methods

Reference and design
Let N denote the number of complete evaluable minutes of one recording. A minute's reference y_i is its annotated AF duration in seconds divided by 60. The finite target is the average of y_i over all N minutes. Let N_h and n_h denote the number of eligible and reviewed minutes in stratum h and let W_h = N_h/N. For a fixed detector and recording, the six strata are determined from the entire raw score clock before restriction to eligible minutes. A high transition flag means that the score differs by at least 0.5 from the previous or next complete clock minute. For each nonempty stratum start with min(2,N_h) draws, then assign one draw at a time to the nonsaturated stratum with largest N_h^2/[n_h(n_h+1)] until all 20 draws are allocated. Within-stratum simple random samples are independent. A fully reviewed stratum makes an exact contribution.

Model-assisted estimation
Let p_i be the calibrated score on minute i. Fit the monotone map on the 42 development records, weighting minutes within a record by 1/N. For each simulated review the estimator adds sum_h W_h * mean_sample,h(y_i - p_i) to the known population mean prediction. The sample variance of the untruncated estimate is estimated as V_obs = sum_h W_h^2 (1-n_h/N_h) s_h^2/n_h, using the within-sample residual variance. The same expression with development residual variances gives V_fit. Both variances omit census strata. Development residual variances receive 0.1 record-equivalent weight on the pooled variance and a floor of 0.03^2. The normal approximation uses V_obs. A residual Hoeffding interval uses half-width sqrt{log(20)/2 * sum_h W_h^2 (1+range_h(p_i))^2/n_h}, with the sum over noncensus strata. The two intervals retain the untruncated centre before intersecting endpoints with [0,1].

Finite-population alternatives
Write d(x,p) for Bernoulli KL divergence with the usual conventions for 0 and 1. Remove fully reviewed strata, renormalize weights over the remaining population, and use m_h for the sampled mean reference and theta_h for an unknown population mean. The direct KL interval contains theta such that d(sum_h W_h m_h,theta) <= max_h(W_h/n_h) log(20). For the projected interval the joint region is sum_h n_h d(m_h,theta_h) <= log(10) + sum_h n_h d(m_h,q_h), with q_h the full-stratum mean prediction clipped to [0.01,0.99] before review. The interval endpoints are the minimum and maximum of sum_h W_h theta_h on the joint region. For either alternative, rescale by the remaining population fraction and add the known contribution of census strata. The study archive gives derivations, numerical implementation and finite-population tests. Performance measures refer to draws retaining the sampling design and full reference ascertainment for all selected minutes.

Empirical radii and interpretation
Clip the corrected estimate to [0,1]. A fixed radius uses the calibration quantile of its absolute error. For adaptation use the quantile of absolute error divided by max(0.001, sqrt[(V_obs+V_fit)/2]). Multiply this quantile by the current review's scale. A new recording and the 42 calibration recordings have equal weight and the new recording's mass is initially placed at positive infinity. At 90% with 1,000 draws per recording, the quantile is the 38,700th finite score in ascending order. Before intersecting with [0,1], fixed half-widths are 5.523 and 7.422 percentage points for ArNet2 and COSEn respectively. Adaptive multipliers are 1.202 and 1.424. Intersections with [0,1] explain why even a fixed radius produces different full widths in the results.

Aggregation
Within each recording, summarize 1,000 repeated checks, then take the arithmetic mean across recordings in the same patient and give patients equal weights. No patient-level linkage is available for the US development and short-recording cohorts. The 95% intervals for aggregate summaries resample patients (or MIT-BIH recording IDs) 10,000 times conditional on the calibration, with identical sampled IDs for paired contrasts. The number of independent patients remains the number of distinct patients in that cohort.
